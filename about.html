A little about me

I started out in biotechnology, found my way into a neurotechnology startup, and became increasingly interested in the people on the other side of the technology.

What makes someone trust a recommendation? Why does a product fit neatly into one person's routine and fall apart in another's? How do you turn a pile of user feedback, data, and half-formed ideas into something a team can actually act on?

Those questions led me to customer experience, product operations, and eventually an MSc in Behavioural Science at the University of Glasgow.

I like work that lets me move between asking questions and getting my hands dirty: talking to users, investigating patterns, shaping reports, building workflows, or helping a team work out what to try next.

I'm especially interested in health, wearables, human-centred technology, and early-stage teams where the problem is still taking shape.

Outside work, I collect interests, open too many tabs, fall into research rabbit holes, and maintain the optimistic belief that I'll eventually get through my reading list.

What's your superpower?

I connect dots across people, products, and systems. Give me a messy problem, a few unanswered questions, and a spreadsheet, and I'll probably find something worth investigating.

What do you want to learn?

I want to get better at turning behavioural insights into products that actually change something, and keep exploring the space between human behaviour, technology, and design.
