Out in the World

A running record of the rooms I've walked into, the people I've learned from, and the ideas I've carried back out. Conferences, startup meetings, webinars, coffee chats and academic events.

Some of my learning has happened in research conferences and university classrooms. Some has happened on startup exhibition floors, during fieldwork, in conversations with founders, or while trying to make a complicated product work in the real world.

I'm interested in the overlap between human behaviour, technology, research and the things people are trying to build. This is where I keep track of what I'm exploring beyond the projects themselves.

January 2025, Las Vegas, USA

CES 2025: Building the experience around the technology

I helped bring Neurostellar's presence at Eureka Park to life, working across booth design, marketing materials, experience design and product demonstrations.

The thread I took away: Product experience starts before someone becomes a user. First impressions, explanations and demonstrations are part of the product itself.

What stayed with me

A technology product has to make sense to someone encountering it for the first time. The experience around the device, the way it's demonstrated and the story told about it all shape how people understand what it can do.

October 2024, Dubai, UAE

GITEX Global 2024: When the demo doesn't go to plan

I represented Neurostellar within a startup portfolio associated with the Tamil Nadu IT Ministry, coordinating exhibition delivery and conducting live product demonstrations after weeks of preparation.

During the six-day event, I also had to deal with setbacks on the exhibition floor.

The thread I took away: Good product work includes the unglamorous part: troubleshooting, communicating clearly and keeping things moving when something breaks.

What stayed with me

Preparation matters, but so does the ability to respond when reality stops following the plan. A live demonstration is simultaneously a product experience, a communication challenge and an operational one.

June 2026, Birmingham, UK

BCBR 2026: Behaviour change beyond the textbook

I attended BCBR 2026 while completing my MSc in Behavioural Science at the University of Glasgow, with interests spanning AI and digital behaviour, health and wellbeing, and sustainability.

The thread I took away: Behavioural science becomes especially interesting when its insights have to survive contact with real products, real decisions and real-world constraints.

What I wanted to explore

How behavioural research moves from explaining what people do to informing what we build, how we communicate and how we design interventions.

Neurostellar fieldwork

Out of the lab, into the game

My work exploring neurotechnology for elite performers took me into chess environments, where I coordinated with coaches, researchers, technical teams, participants and partners.

The thread I took away: Sometimes a new product opportunity starts with learning a world you don't yet understand, rather than trying to fit people into the world your product already knows.

What made this different

The opportunity wasn't just to introduce an existing product to another audience. It involved understanding a specialised performance context and exploring whether neurotechnology could offer something relevant within it.

Academic outreach

Sharing the technology story

I've also spoken to an audience of more than 100 people through an SSN Tech Grad School talk.

The thread I took away: Understanding something and being able to explain it clearly are two different skills. Both matter when technology needs to make sense beyond the people who build it.

What this represents

Communicating ideas to a larger audience is a different exercise from working through the details with a product team. You have to decide what matters, what needs explaining and how to make a complex subject accessible.
